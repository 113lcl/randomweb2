Glass Pavilion

A private gallery of glass and timber, built to hold light rather than keep it out.

Location
Kyoto
Completed
2024
Area
290 m²
Materials
Glass, cedar, steel
Duration
18 months
The brief

A gallery that changes with the weather

Commissioned to house a private collection of contemporary sculpture, the brief asked for a building that would never compete with the art — yet never feel neutral either. The answer was a single-storey glass volume wrapped in a cedar screen that filters and diffuses daylight across the day.

The screen's 4,200 individually angled slats were fabricated at our Carrara workshop and shipped in eleven sealed crates. Each panel rotates two degrees from its neighbour, tracing the sun's path so that no single sculpture receives direct light for more than forty minutes a day.

Beneath the floor, a shallow reflecting pool doubles as passive cooling — drawing heat from the glass roof during Kyoto's humid summers without a single visible vent.

Collectors who visit ask more questions about the building than the work inside it. I've stopped minding. The pavilion taught me to look at light the way I look at a painting.

Sato K.
Collector, Glass Pavilion
