The Cliff House

Built into the edge of a Big Sur bluff, engineered to hold against wind loads most houses never see.

Location
Big Sur
Completed
2021
Area
460 m²
Materials
Rammed earth, glass, cedar
Duration
30 months
The brief

Engineered first for wind, then for anything else

A coastal bluff this exposed dictates the design before aesthetics enter the conversation. Structural wind-tunnel testing came before a single floor plan, and the house that resulted is low, wide and deeply anchored — rammed-earth walls up to ninety centimetres thick on the seaward face, thinning to conventional framing only on the sheltered, landward side.

Every pane of glass on the ocean elevation is laminated to withstand wind-borne debris, set in frames rated for gusts the local code doesn't require but the site's own weather station data made hard to ignore. Cedar cladding, left to weather to grey within eighteen months by design, was chosen because it would need no maintenance regime a family could realistically keep up on a cliff edge three hours from the nearest hardware store.

The result reads as a bunker only in section drawings. In person, deep roof overhangs and a sunken central courtyard make it feel, on a calm day, almost casual — which is exactly the trick a house built for a hundred-year storm is supposed to pull off.

We've been through two real storms since moving in. Both times we forgot to worry, which for a house standing on a cliff edge is the highest compliment I know how to give an engineer.

Robert & Anna T.
Owners, The Cliff House
