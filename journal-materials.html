What we learned from a decade of cutting our own stone

Amara Osei
January 2026

In 2015 we sent a drawing to a supplier for a single curved stone bench. What came back three months later was technically correct and completely wrong — the curve was there, the soul wasn't. That bench is the reason we opened a workshop in Carrara instead of writing sterner specification documents.

Cutting our own stone didn't make projects cheaper. It made them slower, in fact, by an average of six weeks per commission. What it bought us was the ability to stand next to the blade and say "again" — something no email thread to a supplier three time zones away has ever achieved.

A material has a memory

Stone quarried from a single seam carries the same veining logic across every block. We now select entire slabs for a project from one seam, sequentially, so a kitchen island and the bathroom floor three rooms away are visibly cut from the same piece of mountain. Clients rarely name this consciously. They feel it as coherence.

“The workshop taught us more about proportion in a year than five years of drafting had.”

Timber followed a similar path in 2019, when we began kiln-drying our own oak and cedar rather than buying pre-finished stock. Raw boards move, check and reveal grain in ways finished lumber never shows you until it's already on a wall.

What this costs a client

Honestly — time, mostly. Bespoke fabrication adds four to ten weeks to most schedules compared with off-the-shelf finishes. We tell every client this in the first meeting, before they fall in love with a sample they can't have on the original timeline. Almost everyone chooses to wait anyway.
