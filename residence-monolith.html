The Monolith

A single fold of concrete cut into an Aspen ridge, engineered to read as one continuous surface.

Location
Aspen
Completed
2022
Area
810 m²
Materials
Concrete, glass, bronze
Duration
40 months
The brief

One pour, one line, no visible seams

The client, a sculptor, wanted a house that behaved like a single object rather than an assembly of rooms. The answer was a board-formed concrete shell poured in continuous sections, its roofline folding down to meet the ridge at one end and cantilevering nine metres over the valley at the other — no visible seam breaks the surface from entry to edge.

Achieving that took four separate pour campaigns across two winters, each one referencing the exact grain and colour of the last so the join would be invisible once the formwork came off. Patinated bronze window frames were chosen specifically because they would weather at roughly the same rate as the concrete, so the two materials age together rather than one outlasting the other.

Inside, the same discipline continues: not a single visible seam between floor, wall and ceiling in the main living volume, achieved by casting radiators, lighting and structure into the concrete itself before the pour.

I make objects for a living, and I still can't point to where one pour ends and the next begins. OBSIDIAN built me the only sculpture I'll ever live inside.

Daniel W.
Owner, The Monolith
