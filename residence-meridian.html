Villa Meridian

Terraced into an olive grove above the Mediterranean, following the fall of the land rather than fighting it.

Location
French Riviera
Completed
2023
Area
540 m²
Materials
Limestone, glass, teak
Duration
26 months
The brief

Three terraces, four generations of olive trees

The site came with a working olive grove planted by the current owner's great-grandfather, and the one non-negotiable in the brief was that not a single tree would move. The villa is instead split across three stepped terraces that thread between the existing rows, each level roughly four metres below the one above it, following the original agricultural terracing rather than a poured foundation grid.

Local limestone, quarried from a site eleven kilometres away, forms the retaining walls and continues inside as flooring — the same stone that has always held these hillsides up now holds the house up too. Teak louvres, salvaged from a decommissioned fishing vessel in Marseille, shade the western terrace against the harshest of the afternoon sun.

A drip-irrigation line for the grove runs beneath the lowest terrace, tied into the same water system as the house — the olive harvest, now pressed on-site each autumn, was never meant to be a design feature, but it's become one anyway.

Every other architect we spoke to wanted to clear the grove for a flat build plate. OBSIDIAN was the only one who asked how old the trees were before asking anything about square metres.

Claire M.
Owner, Villa Meridian
